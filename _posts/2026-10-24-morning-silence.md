when i was a better selves fellow at knoll farm in 2017, one of the practices we engaged in was a morning silence practice. my friend and mentor, peter forbes, was the offerer of the fellowship and this practice and it has been transformative. full stop. i could say so much about how it impacted me during that fateful week almost ten years ago, how i have used it in my life since, how and where i have shared the insights from it in other places, and on and on. 

but in this moment, the thing i want to share is that this summer i found a new metaphor (analogy? simile? my middle school english class is failing me) to explain the practice. here's the core of it:

> deep down, i believe that each and every one of us is a gift. and, for many reasons, some good and some less good, most of us give ourselves away to others before we give ourselves to ourselves. practicing morning silence is a way to give the gift of you to you first. 

now, i love me a good metaphor so i could really dive off the deep end with the metaphoric extensions here (which i sometimes when i share this practice during an active facilitation moment). instead of doing that though, i just want to touch on the "good and not so good reasons" for giving ourselves away to others before we give ourselves to ourselves. 

some of the good reasons:

* we parent
* we are partnered with folks who we love and want/need us
* culturally, reciprocity is the norm and it's normal to share yourself with others because others will be doing that with you in a way that feels balanced and restorative 

some of the less good reasons:

* we are in family or partner dynamics where other people's demands/needs are valued higher than our own
* we have to work in such a way that there is no space to be with ourselves
* we have been trained/conditioned to believe that we aren't valuable outside of our service to others
* cell phones

there are more reasons on both of these lists but i wanted to touch on a few that i see or have known myself to illustrate how many of us these reasons impact. if any of those reasons resonate with you, know that you're not alone. 

in this moment, i don't have time/space to share more about the practice itself but what i can say at the moment is this: if you want to try it, all you have to do is pick a time (or event, like breakfast) after which you'll break your silence and then just be silent from the time you wake up until then. if you live in a multi-person context, you might have to let some folks that's what you're doing but i bet that more likely than not, if you try it, you'll see what's so powerful about it. 

ok that's it for now. thanks for coming to my ted talk, haha.
