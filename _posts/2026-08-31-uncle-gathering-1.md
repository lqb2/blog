so i'm just home from spending a fun lil night out at the inaugural fall gathering of uncles! a fun detail: it was a party of one! i was gonna go to the saloon but it was a beautiful evening so i got a cocktail at cambridge common and sat outside. 

earlier today i looked up a few uncle listicles and felt excited to dive into them. the overall theme of the few i picked was 'uncles in pop culture.' 

i got settled in and opened the first one: [Say Uncle: Top Ten Uncles in Media](https://erichagmannmusic.com/2023/07/26/say-uncle-top-ten-uncles-in-media/). it ended up being the only one i got into because my friend called me back from earlier in the day and then i got to do a lil video chat with her and her 1 year old (sky, one of my newest nibblings)! even with that, the time i spent in the article was juicy. 

two things i want to report back on:

first, [this scene from the fresh prince of bel air](https://www.youtube.com/watch?app=desktop&is=-M12woXrZYKd26Gj&v=ZbWpdEA-R3o&feature=youtu.be). i had fully forgotten how powerful this scene was. uncle phil! what a boss. 

second, an insight that came through while watching that clip and seeing the uncles on the list: there is some cultural fascination with uncles who act as father figures. specifically, there's a centering of uncles who replace fathers. 

at a later point this evening, i had a completely unrelated phone chat with erwin, someone who has come to some of the uncle sessions abraham and i have been gathering. he and i both recognized the same thing: that's not really what i'm talking about when i talk about uncling. i'm much more interested in uncles as additional caregivers, not substitute ones. 

there's something about this replacement thing that smells a bit like the classic hero narrative... not a fan. 

anyway, that's all i want to say for now. gathering number #1 in the books! excited about the next one!
