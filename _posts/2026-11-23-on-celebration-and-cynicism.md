a couple weeks ago while prepping for a facilitation gig, i was in a conversation with my co-facilitator, yoojin lee, and we were discussing the prevailing reactions/responses to the nov 3 election "blue wave." the following thoughts came out pretty baked so i'm just taking a second to share them because i think it's v relevant. 

the election results were stunningly positive for democrats (and you might even say the left broadly). 

on the day of, i spent a good amount of time crying and being excited for all the different ways different results came back in our favor/on our side. 

and somehow, in spite of that, over the following days, the overall vibe was less celebratory than it hought it would be. some folks were clearly less celebratory than others (and tbh i even had my own touch of this).

of course, there could be many reasons for this. but the one i want to drill in on for a bit is cynicism. 

now, my experience with cynicism is that it is a protective stance. sometimes it appears because you’re protecting yourself from disappointment. in some similar past parallel experience, things didn’t go as great as you had hoped and that was disappointing. so the cynicism keeps you from getting to excited so that the disappointment feels smaller this time. 

that disappointment in those past experiences is often connected to or is an element of grief. 

my work with grief over the past few years has taught me that if we don't grieve those past experiences, they will hang with us longer than we need. 

what i wish we could do (and we still can) is really grieve those past experiences. moving the energy of that grief can allow us to release what happened last time so we can be with what's happening this time. moving that grief is really, really important. and if we don't do it, we are actually more likely to recreate the same situation now as the one we're grieving because we won't take the action needed this time while stuck in our old feelings. 

if we can do this grieving, i think it could be totally possible to fully celebrate the victories of this moment **and** be realistic. celebrating now doesn't mean all is won or that we can sit back and let the system operate on its own. 

more generally, what i hope we can learn this round is that it's so possible to be happy for and celebrate particular moments and know that any single or series of wins isn't the whole story (if you really think about it, we're talking about 400 years of a nation state project that is gonna take a while to shift to where we want it). 

not celebrating now both keeps us from feeling the good feelings that come from the recognition and acknowledgement that hard work *can* lead to real outcomes. tbh, it also makes us a hard team to make other people excited to join. if we can't celebrate even when we win, energetically, why would someone (especially someone who was neutral or on the other side) be excited to join us? 

celebrating victories does not mean we must succumb to magical thinking. we can be happy now and still keep our eyes on the work to come, ya know?
